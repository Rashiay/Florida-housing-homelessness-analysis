# Housing Landscape: Supply, Demand, and Socioeconomic Impact

A Power BI data analysis examining Florida's housing market, income trends, supply-demand disparities, and homelessness from 2010 to 2023.

---

## Project Overview & Goal

Florida is experiencing rapid population expansion, but housing availability and affordability have not kept pace. The goal of this project is to analyze how home sales, household income, and vacant housing stock intersect with homelessness, evaluating whether Florida is building enough homes to support its residents.

---

## Report Structure & Findings

### Page 1: Home Sales and Household Income
* Analyzes median household income alongside total new home sales by price tier.
* **Key Finding:** Entry-level housing under $300,000 has drastically shrunk, concentrating most sales in the $300,000–$399,999 range. Declines in median household income (notably in 2022) combined with rising price floors create severe financial barriers for lower-income buyers.

### Page 2: Homeless Individuals in FL and How This Translates to the Housing Market
* Compares total homeless populations against Florida's home vacancy rates over time.
* **Key Finding:** While homeless counts have fluctuated (peaking in 2011 at 37,584 and rebounding to 22,925 in 2023), home vacancy rates have steadily dropped. Evaluating vacant units relative to unhoused individuals reveals that existing vacant housing stock represents an underutilized leverage point for intervention.

### Page 3: Is Florida Building Enough Homes for Our People?
* Evaluates year-over-year population growth against housing development to complete the full-circle analysis.
* **Key Finding:** Population growth consistently outpaces new housing unit construction, particularly visible during spike years like 2015 and 2021.

---

## Main Conclusion

Our analysis demonstrates that **Florida faces a multi-layered housing crisis**. Solving it requires a holistic approach built on three core pillars:
1. **Building enough homes** to keep up with population growth.
2. **Distributing housing fairly** so that inventory under $300,000 remains available to average income earners.
3. **Targeting those in need** by leveraging existing vacant housing stock to reduce homelessness.
